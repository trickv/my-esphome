SENSOR BEHAVIOR SUMMARY
------------------------
The AS3935 chip has a multi-stage detection pipeline:
  1. Watchdog threshold - signal must exceed this to be considered at all
  2. Pattern matching - compares received waveform against expected 500kHz
     lightning signature (~600us exponential decay envelope)
  3. Spike rejection - further filters narrow transients

Our sensor reliably detects lightning electromagnetic pulses (correlates
tightly with Blitzortung ground-truth data) but almost always classifies
them as "disturbers" rather than "lightning." The native lightning
classification is unreliable and generates false positives.

DETECTION RATE ANALYSIS
-----------------------
For Blitzortung-confirmed strikes within 25km, we checked whether the
sensor produced ANY event (disturber or lightning) within 2 seconds:

  Distance band    | Blitz strikes | Sensor detected | Detection rate
  -----------------+---------------+-----------------+---------------
  < 10 km          |    171        |    141           |   82.5%
  10-20 km         |    492        |    368           |   74.8%
  20-25 km         |    392        |    286           |   73.0%
  Overall < 25km   |  1,055        |    795           |   75.4%

Key finding: the sensor detects ~75-82% of real lightning strikes as
measured by Blitzortung, but classifies them ALL as "disturber" rather
than "lightning." The sensor's RF front-end is working. The pattern
matcher is the problem.


CLASSIFICATION ANALYSIS
-----------------------
Of 65 total "lightning" classifications the chip produced:
  - Most occurred AFTER reorienting the PCB (see Physical Findings below)
  - Most occurred at minimum thresholds (noise=1, watchdog=1, spike=1)
  - 0% had Blitzortung correlation within +/-10 seconds
  - These are FALSE POSITIVES: the chip fires "lightning" on signals that
    do NOT correspond to real lightning

Hourly sensor event breakdown:
  Hour (UTC)  | Disturbers | Lightning | Notes
  ------------+------------+-----------+------
  02:00       |   1,263    |     0     | Storm arriving, thresholds at 2/2/2
  03:00       |   2,923    |     0     | Peak storm, all thresholds
  04:00       |   8,396    |    17     | Post-reorientation + min thresholds
  05:00       |   9,700    |    31     | Tapering storm, min thresholds
  06:00       |   6,553    |    17     | Storm gone, false positives
  07:00+      |    ~100    |     0     | Thresholds restored to 2/2/2

The lightning classifications at 04:00-06:00 correlated with lowered
thresholds (noise=1, watchdog=1) and PCB reorientation. However, they
did NOT correlate with actual Blitzortung strikes, making them false
positives. Restoring thresholds to noise=2, watchdog=2, spike=2
eliminated the false positives.


ANTENNA TUNING
--------------
We performed a capacitance sweep using ESPHome's pulse_counter sensor
to measure the LCO output frequency on the IRQ pin during tune_antenna
mode. Division ratio confirmed as 16 (AS3935 default).

  Capacitance (index) | Added pF | Measured (Hz) | LCO freq (kHz) | Error vs 500kHz
  --------------------+----------+---------------+----------------+----------------
  0  (0 pF)           |    0     |    31,298     |    500.77      |   +0.15%
  8  (64 pF)          |   64     |    31,210     |    499.36      |   -0.13%
  15 (120 pF)         |  120     |    30,656     |    490.50      |   -1.90%

Conclusions:
  - Antenna is within spec at ALL capacitance values (3.5% tolerance)
  - Best tuning is capacitance=0 (500.77 kHz, +0.15% error)
  - Tuning was NEVER the cause of misclassification
  - The sweep is monotonic (freq decreases as cap increases, as expected)
  - Total swing across full range is ~2%, suggesting the antenna has
    significant parasitic capacitance beyond the tunable 0-120pF

PHYSICAL FINDINGS
-----------------
PCB orientation matters significantly:
  - Before reorientation: PCB leaning on its short edge, antenna bar
    axis pointing horizontally toward/away from the wall
  - After reorientation: PCB standing upright, antenna bar (under white
    sticker at top of SparkFun board) horizontal, perpendicular to wall
  - The reorientation coincided with the first-ever "lightning"
    classification, though these turned out to be false positives

The SparkFun AS3935 board has a ferrite bar antenna under a white
sticker along the top edge. Ferrite bar antennas are most sensitive
along their long axis. For lightning (horizontal magnetic field),
the bar should be horizontal.

Moving the sensor from indoors to the front porch made no difference
in classification behavior (still all disturbers) but did increase
the event rate (fewer missed events due to building attenuation).


BLITZORTUNG INTEGRATION NOTES
------------------------------
The comparison tool (lightning-vs-blitzortung.py) connects to the
Blitzortung WebSocket at wss://ws8.blitzortung.org/.

IMPORTANT: The WebSocket protocol changed (as of 2026). Key details:
  - Subscription message: {"a": 111} (global feed, NOT a bounding box)
  - Payloads are LZW-compressed and must be decoded client-side
  - Filter by radius client-side using haversine
  - Some servers (ws3-6, ws9-10) have SSL cert issues
  - Connection drops silently without error; reconnect logic needed

The decode_blitzortung() function in the script handles the LZW
decompression. See the script source for implementation.


SOFTWARE LIGHTNING DETECTION (RECOMMENDED APPROACH)
---------------------------------------------------
Since the AS3935's native classification is unreliable, we implemented
a software-based approach in the ESPHome firmware:

  1. Logger on_message trigger catches AS3935 "Disturber was detected"
  2. Two-bucket rolling counter: current bucket + previous bucket
  3. Every 30 seconds, current rolls to previous, current resets
  4. "Disturber Rate" template sensor published to HA (events per window)
  5. "Lightning Nearby" binary sensor fires when rate >= 10

This works because:
  - 75-82% of real strikes produce a sensor event (classified as disturber)
  - During active storms, disturber rate jumps from ~0/min to 100-300+/min
  - False quiet-weather disturber rate is very low (~2-4/hour)
  - The rate-based approach is robust against individual false events

Threshold of 10 per 30s window was chosen to avoid false triggers from
occasional appliance noise while remaining sensitive enough to detect
storms with strikes at 25-50km range.


RECOMMENDED PHYSICAL NEXT STEPS
--------------------------------
If you want to improve the native AS3935 lightning classification
(beyond the software workaround), consider these in priority order:

1. TRY A DIFFERENT AS3935 MODULE
   The SparkFun AS3935 (and most cheap modules) are known in the
   community for unreliable pattern matching. The DFRobot Gravity
   AS3935 module reportedly has better analog front-end quality.
   Same chip, different board/antenna design. Cost: ~$30.

2. EXTERNAL ANTENNA
   The SparkFun board supports an external antenna via the ANT jumper.
   A properly-designed external 500kHz loop or ferrite rod antenna
   with better Q factor could produce a cleaner waveform that the
   pattern matcher accepts. Would require:
   - Cut the onboard antenna trace (or move the solder jumper)
   - Connect an external antenna to the ANT pad
   - Re-tune capacitance after antenna change

3. DEDICATED OUTDOOR ENCLOSURE
   Build a weather-resistant enclosure for permanent porch mounting.
   Keep the sensor oriented with the ferrite bar horizontal. Use a
   linear (non-switching) USB power supply or add a 220uF+ capacitor
   across the power rails to reduce power supply noise that could
   corrupt waveforms during the detection window.

4. SECOND SENSOR FOR COMPARISON
   Run two AS3935 modules simultaneously (different ESPHome nodes).
   If one classifies lightning correctly and the other doesn't, that
   confirms a chip-level defect. If both behave identically, it
   points to environmental factors.

5. CUSTOM FIRMWARE CLASSIFICATION
   Instead of relying on the AS3935's built-in pattern matcher,
   capture the raw IRQ timing and energy data and implement a
   custom classifier. This would be an ESPHome custom component
   that overrides the AS3935 loop() to:
   - Record timestamps of ALL events (disturber + lightning)
   - Compute inter-event timing patterns
   - Apply a trained model (even a simple heuristic) to classify
     bursts as "storm activity" vs "local noise"
   This is effectively what the software binary_sensor already does,
   but could be more sophisticated.
